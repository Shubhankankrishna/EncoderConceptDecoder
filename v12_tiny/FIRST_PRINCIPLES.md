## B. What our evidence says (do not re-litigate)

1. Text/arith/Sudoku at ~0.5M: concept path never beats C0; visit 2 inert; codes produce no different futures
   (mixture − committed ≤ 0.002 nats); NCP forecast worse than its own input; bridge harmful (2.6–2.9× NTP grad).
2. Fixed 8-token groups create layout entanglement (period-8 Sudoku signature in V10.3 and V11).
3. V5 (2M, GraphNav switching colour): posterior Z had authority over the frozen decoder, but β saturated
   (ungated) or recall 0.29 (gated), and internal RL < prior mean, because the latent was copyable (4-back) and
   segments were ~4 hops; plain sampling already succeeded 35–89% — no exploration bottleneck.
4. RBF bank (frozen weights) −1.55 nats on an unseen module through concept routes only; consolidation into
   gated LoRA keeps ~90% with zero forgetting on dissimilar families; related skill (add/sub) unprotected.

## C. First principles

P1. A concept/controller variable can only earn its keep where the data has a latent variable that
    (a) persists over many tokens, (b) is not recoverable by copying a recent token, (c) is multimodal given the
    prefix (several plausible next subgoals), and (d) composes. Text at 0.5M does not expose such a variable
    to a model that cannot yet model text. => V12's test bed must contain such a family, generated so that
    segment lengths are *variable and not aligned* to any fixed group size (user's V9 bias rule).

P2. Regressing a multimodal next-concept with MSE returns the conditional mean (XM §2.1). A *discrete*
    segment codebook with **cross-entropy over code indices** is natively multimodal (categorical =
    generative expressivity up to N per segment). So the codebook should be the *target alphabet* of NCP
    (predict next code indices by CE), not a soft-mixture regressor. That is the principled reason to keep the
    discrete codebook the user is bullish on. XM then only needs to cover the continuous residual, if any.

P3. NCP's target should be something the decoder needs. V11 predicted the next *encoder* pooled state; the
    user's intuition (HiLP-style) is that a concept head should predict the *decoder's* future latent. HiLP's
    upper level is exactly u_t → u_{t+k} on the token-model's own states, with stop-grad target, small weight,
    and no token input. Predicting sg[pool(decoder states of the next segment)] from the concept state is a
    HiLP upper-level loss whose target is grounded by NTP (cannot collapse while NTP holds).

P4. NextLat at token rate on the decoder (d=1, MLP, SmoothL1 + frozen-head KL, stop-grads) is the cheap,
    well-tested belief-state shaper. It belongs on the decoder, *not* routed through the concept (V11's bridge
    was a concept-anchored 8-step rollout without re-grounding — that is what pushed 2.6–2.9× NTP's gradient).

P5. The metacontroller must be a *second phase on a frozen base* (paper Fig. 7 + our own co-training failure).
    The ECD concept module is the natural mid-depth insertion point (paper Fig. 4: mid-depth control is best).
    Two insertion options to compare: (i) token-rate controller at the decoder input / mid-depth residual
    (paper-faithful); (ii) concept-rate controller on the concept stream. Fixed groups would force switches onto
    group boundaries; learned β does not. Test whether concept-rate control loses switch precision.

P6. The learned switch β replaces fixed chunking *for the controller*. Whether the encoder's pooling should
    also become dynamic (boundaries from β or from an entropy/cosine boundary detector, H-Net style) is a
    second-order question; first measure whether fixed pooling hurts on variable-length segments.

P7. Loop-of-2 earns its compute only if visit 2 sees something visit 1 did not. Candidate: visit 2 is the
    *controlled* visit (metacontroller writes U on visit-2 input) or the *hypothesis* visit (explorative code).
    Otherwise drop it (evidence: inert everywhere).

P8. Success criteria must match the mechanism: probes (subgoal decodability by depth), ground-truth-controller
    compositional success (paper Fig. 4), rate–distortion gap + switch F1 (Fig. 7), internal-RL vs raw-token RL
    success on a sparse-reward recombination task (Fig. 8). NLL is a guard, not the goal.

P9. Continual learning: fast RBF bank (hippocampus) → sleep consolidation into gated low-rank experts (cortex)
    via distillation on *self-generated* dreams, then reset the bank (Sleep §3.2–3.3). Dreams can be generated
    by sampling metacontroller codes z ~ N(0,I) (the analogue of Sleep's random-expert routing for novelty).
    Related-skill forgetting needs replay or a task-separating gate (concept-state gate measured to overlap —
    re-measure on the new family).

## D. Proposed test plan shape (to be reviewed)

Phase 1: pretrain ~1–3M-param bases on a token-form "pinpad" compositional subgoal world (+ a small text
fraction as guard), arms: plain transformer (same size), ECD+NCP-CE, ECD+NCP-CE+HiLP-decoder-latent,
ECD+NextLat(decoder) ... Phase 2: probes + GT controllers + metacontroller α sweep on frozen bases.
Phase 3: internal RL vs raw-token RL on the held-out composition. Phase 4: sleep (RBF → gated LoRA) on a new
subgoal/new colour. ~12 h on the M2 Air.
