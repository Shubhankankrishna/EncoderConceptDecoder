# V8 tiny audit — 6 October 2026

## Verdict

The running process is numerically healthy, but useful generalization has **not** been established. Fixed-instance memorization passed. Fresh edge lookup and two-hop composition remain weak. The competence-first queue prevents the main V7 failure of refitting and searching an already incompetent backbone, but this implementation also contains concrete data, validation and budget gaps. It is not ready to support a claim that the complete V8 design has been tested correctly.

This audit read all 15 executable Python files, the protocol/design snapshot, locked manifests, preflight artifacts, live logs and relevant V6/V7 training summaries. Independent checks used CPU only; no new training was launched. Source, configuration, data, checkpoints and running processes were not altered. This report is outside the locked executable source.

Observation cutoff: **6 October 2026, 00:40:43 Asia/Calcutta** (5 October 19:10:43 UTC). Scores below are development or engineering observations, not sealed model-quality results.

## What is sound and materially better than V7

- The hierarchy is 2,448,640 learned parameters; the ordinary Transformer is 2,450,048. Parameter matching is close, though compute is not identical because concept blocks process fewer positions.
- Each completed foundation is allocated 24,000 updates and **576,000 GraphNav examples**, plus explicit lookup/composition/pointer quotas. The successful historical V6 prefix logged 288,032 graph instances including its initialization probe; V7 NCP seed 171 logged only 36,713. V8's intended exposure repairs that deficit, but none of its foundation exposure has occurred yet.
- Engineering weights do not initialize the primary models. Absolute competence and uncertainty enter refit/continual promotion; preservation of zero navigation success cannot pass.
- Independent inspection accepted **all 15,360 held-out symbolic references**, using a separate public-header checker as well as the production verifier. Answer masks had the correct next-token shift, document masks were correct, every reference fit its declared context, and all hard goals had shortest distance at least four. All query identities were distinct, matched the exclusion list, and all suite file hashes matched the manifest.
- The current source, data manifest, design snapshot and protocol hashes match `LOCK.json`. A CPU rerun of the current revision's trained-memorization contracts passed all **300 checks**. This establishes those tested contracts, not production Metal compilation or model quality.
- FineWeb-Edu base and continual partitions had no identical token documents across train/dev/test within each family. The findings below concern TinyStories.

## Findings that need correction or a narrower claim

### 1. TinyStories has content leakage despite disjoint document IDs

**Confirmed data bug.** [data.py:38](code/data.py#L38) partitions document IDs without grouping identical token content. Hashing actual document tokens found:

| Overlap | Identical content shared across partitions |
|---|---:|
| Train/development | 112 unique documents |
| Train/test | 99 unique documents |
| Development/test | 1 unique document |

The fixed 256-row TinyStories development evaluation includes **16 contaminated rows / 3,013 of 46,588 scored targets**. Its test evaluation includes **19 contaminated rows / 3,453 of 46,599 targets**. Models have not reached main text training yet; these are accessible training copies, not evidence that those copies have already been sampled.

The setup therefore violates the draft's exact token-content deduplication requirement. TinyStories evaluation is not fully held out as currently defined. This does not explain the current symbolic failure, and it does not contaminate the independently checked symbolic queries.

**Required remedy before reliable primary text results:** group exact token-content hashes before partitioning, regenerate a versioned dataset/manifest and lock, and repeat affected validation. Preserve this run's provenance; do not silently replace data under its existing lock. Near-duplicate handling remains a separate documented question.

### 2. Engineering uses a different Q optimizer from the foundation

**Confirmed recipe mismatch.** [runtime.py:63](code/runtime.py#L63) gives Q the 10× LR/no-decay group only for `A` and `HARD`. `MEM` and `SIMPLE` still use VQ loss and dead-code restarts, but Q receives **1× LR and weight decay 0.1**. This alternative is not clearly declared as a separate Q-LR engineering ablation.

Consequently, failure of the current learning ladder cannot establish that the intended foundation optimizer is unlearnable. The mismatch is a plausible contributing factor, not a proven explanation. One fresh CPU diagnostic batch at update 2,000 assigned approximately 10% of squared gradient norm to Q; that single batch does not prove sustained clipping interference or causality.

**Remedy:** align the engineering optimizer with the intended foundation recipe, or explicitly study the difference. Keep the present result as a separate engineering observation rather than attributing it to NCP, which is disabled here.

### 3. Accepted downstream versions lack complete sealed testing

**Confirmed coverage gap.** [drive.py:193](code/drive.py#L193) enumerates original foundations and only the final accepted anchor of each continual trajectory. It does not guarantee separate sealed evaluations of the accepted hard continuation, replacement, exploration model, or every intermediate promoted continual version. Search has its own hard10 test subset if its gate passes; that is not a full backbone/interface test of each endpoint.

Retention in [evaluate.py:79](code/evaluate.py#L79) constrains standard and lookup success plus two text losses. It explicitly skips hard10, composition and pointer outcomes. A replacement or continual candidate could therefore lose useful hard-task ability while passing the implemented retention rule. Hard continuation promotion itself requires preservation but no hard-task gain/gate ([drive.py:155](code/drive.py#L155)); this relaxation is disclosed in the pilot protocol but is narrower than the full draft.

**Remedy:** register each accepted endpoint; run paired sealed baseline/candidate measurements for the component claims; enforce hard-task retention when the anchor has useful hard competence. Report unresolved bounds as unresolved. Do not equate final-trajectory testing with testing every promoted version.

### 4. The 12-hour ceiling is not enforced across all work

**Confirmed budget-control gap, not an observed overrun.** The driver checks estimated availability before dispatch, but its child-wait loop does not check the global deadline ([drive.py:26](code/drive.py#L26)). Trainers have time checks between updates; evaluation, density fitting and inline measurements do not have equivalent end-to-end deadlines.

Search calibration and its 96 development tuning requests run before any overall elapsed-time check ([search_experiment.py:17](code/search_experiment.py#L17)). Only the later test loop checks `seconds`. If per-request budget is `b`, tuning alone can approach `96*b` seconds, plus calibration and final-attempt overhead. Per-candidate late-success exclusion is correct, but it does not bound the whole experiment stage.

Stage estimates are based on one hierarchy training benchmark and reused for a slower token-level flat model and other distinct paths. They are useful admission estimates, not a measured guarantee for each stage. The recorded deadline, **12:18:36 IST**, is therefore a scheduling target rather than a strict all-stage watchdog.

**Remedy:** propagate a common absolute deadline through fitting/evaluation/tuning, check it between bounded units, and calibrate each production stage. Preserve finite checkpoints and report partial work. Search currently comes after both continual trajectories, so completing every idea inside 12 hours is particularly uncertain.

### 5. Preflight provenance and trained production-cache coverage are incomplete

**Validation gap.** The locked contract and stage-gradient artifacts passed on earlier source hashes (`959fec…`, `d4325a…`), whereas the launched revision is `ea636b…`. A whole-code hash difference can arise from harmless reporting edits; it is not proof of a numerical bug. However, there is no recorded per-file change attribution establishing which final paths were covered. The driver checks their pass status rather than requiring tests of the exact current revision.

The new CPU contract rerun covers the current source and trained memorization checkpoint. It does **not** establish final-revision Metal compiled gradient parity. Existing trained-cache checks cover short continuations of one generated 10-node prompt. The queue does not rerun production fast-cache audits after trained Z installation or trained LSL gates, and it does not cover full 384-position graph continuations and active interventions as promised in the full draft.

**Remedy:** bind checks to the final revision (or record and justify relevant file diffs); test trained zero/nonzero Z and installed nonzero LoRA gates under full-prefix, dynamic and production fast decoding, including long continuations and decision margins. An existing cache parity gap is not itself a proven decoding bug.

### 6. Learning diagnostics and comparison breadth remain narrower than the draft

- Primary training emits aggregate domain CE, NCP, VQ and gradient norms. Intermediate primary competence/node-choice screens at the draft's 2k/4k/6k/8k/12k/18k/24k landmarks are absent; development task evaluation occurs after a complete foundation. The engineering ladder helps, but a freshly initialized mixed-task foundation could still develop a new bottleneck unnoticed in aggregate loss.
- The hierarchy's failed simple gate exits before attempting the ordinary Transformer's simple gate ([drive.py:141](code/drive.py#L141)). This saves compute, but a failure cannot then be called a demonstrated shared hierarchy/Transformer recipe failure. Dedicated flat memorization and a freshly trained C2 depth bridge are also absent.
- Current lookup/composition learning is mixed from the start. The planned isolated one-hop ladder/short-path curriculum is not implemented, and task gradient contributions are not separately logged.
- Search quality comparisons all run on the selected hierarchy. The flat model calibrates wall time but does not receive its own retry/search quality curve. This cannot establish a whole-pipeline advantage over ordinary Transformers with comparable total development/training compute.
- Canonical-label comparison, independent trained seeds, post-continual search revalidation, full Z controls and several routing/retention diagnostics are explicitly deferred. Their absence is legitimate pilot scope, but their hypotheses remain untested.

## Decision

Continuing the **bounded current simple-task check** can provide useful learnability evidence. Its 6,000-update cap and 95%/90% stopping gate should remain intact; do not lower them or declare generalization from memorization. At the current rate, roughly 13 minutes of training remained at the observation cutoff, with additional screen/checkpoint overhead; this is an estimate, not a promise.

Before relying on a long primary study, fix content-level text partitioning and align or explicitly isolate the Q optimizer difference. Close the endpoint-testing, deadline and production-validation gaps in a new declared revision. If the ladder fails, diagnose simple retrieval first using an isolated one-hop task, the intended optimizer and a C2/flat control; do not launch refit/Z/MCTS to compensate for failed learning.

This is **useful bounded diagnostic work so far**, not evidence that V8 is doing well scientifically. The V7 exposure and gate-order mistakes are substantially addressed in the plan, but corrected exposure has yet to be executed and other mistakes remain. Neither the present weak checkpoint nor the audit proves that a 2–3M model or the encoder/concept/decoder idea cannot work.
